The regulators split, the labs re-rank, and the price of frontier AI collapses

Twenty-four stories across six beats — data privacy, AI governance, AGI, regulatory compliance, frontier labs, and AI in China — stack-ranked by significance for a fast executive read.

Executive summary

Three currents define today's brief. First, the policy center of gravity is moving to Washington — a June 2 Trump executive order resets US federal AI policy toward a security-first, anti-licensing posture, a bipartisan House draft proposes the first frontier-model audit regime, and a DOJ task force presses to preempt state AI laws — even as the EU quietly delays its AI Act's high-risk deadlines to 2027 while arming its AI Office with turnover-based enforcement powers that go live August 2.

Second, the competitive map has been redrawn. Anthropic's $65B raise at a $965B valuation makes it the world's most valuable AI lab for the first time, ahead of OpenAI — and days later its cheap-but-capable Claude Sonnet 5 launch, GPT-5.6's gated preview, and a wave of near-frontier Chinese open-weight models (Zhipu's MIT-licensed GLM-5.2, Alibaba's Qwen3.7, Moonshot's Kimi K2.6) collapse the cost of frontier-grade capability toward roughly one-fifth of last year's.

Third, the capability debate is maturing. DeepMind is now publishing roadmaps for what comes after AGI and Sam Altman has rebranded OpenAI a "superintelligence company" — yet ARC-AGI-3 shows every frontier model still scoring under 1% on genuinely novel reasoning, a reminder that the gap between benchmark dominance and general intelligence is real. The connective thread for GTM: buyers now have credible, cheaper, more open alternatives, and a compliance clock that starts ticking on August 2.
